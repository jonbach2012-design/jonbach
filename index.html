What I Change
Maverick Spend · Decision Architecture
Buyer finds cheaper supplier outside the framework. System shows no consequence. Organisation loses group discounts, traceability and negotiating position.
TCO visible at the point of decision. The right choice becomes the easy choice — not a compliance requirement.
TCO · Tender Evaluation
Spreadsheet-based tender scoring. Manual weighting. Nobody can explain the decision a year later — or reconstruct which alternatives existed.
Structured scoring with full evidence trail. The decision is traceable, weighted and can explain itself.
TCO · Operational Costs
Inventory binding, production stops and manual ordering flows treated as unavoidable overhead. Nobody has put a number on it.
VMI that surfaces and eliminates hidden operational cost. Automatic replenishment — fewer stops, measurable saving.
TCO · Regulatory Risk
PPWR and EPR treated as legal overhead. Compliance is something that happens after the sourcing decision — not part of it.
Regulation as explicit decision variable. Material choice, supplier choice and risk assessment connected in one system.

Where This Is Going

The next step is not building more systems.
It is building organisations that can build their own.

The risk in being the person who understands procurement, compliance and IT at once is becoming the bottleneck. The more useful long-term position is teaching people to think systemically, question assumptions and build solutions — so the capability stays when the project ends.

This is the direction the work is moving: from solving problems, to building systems that solve them, to building organisations that can do both.

The question I start with
"What is the actual problem — not the symptom?"
Maverick spend, poor supplier selection, hidden TCO — these are symptoms. The work starts by finding the decision architecture failure underneath.
The question I build towards
"If we solve this once, how do we solve it at scale?"
A system that runs. A playbook that travels. A team that no longer needs the specialist in the room to make a defensible decision.
The question that guides the work
"How do we learn from the result?"
Every decision made well is an input to the next one. The goal is not a better answer — it is a better capacity for answering.
